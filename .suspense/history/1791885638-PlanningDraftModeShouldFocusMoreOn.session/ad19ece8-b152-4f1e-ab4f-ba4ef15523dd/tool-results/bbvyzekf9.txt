5:A Planning should be built as a reusable component that will be reused throughout the rest of the codebase.  A component is configurable through arguments or properties that alter its behaviour, appearance, and details.  A Planning should encapsulate all of its own reusable behaviour, while leaving the wiring, integration, and integration to the domain of the application.
17:Planning opens in a tab of its own in the body, "Planning", with a checklist icon, pinned right after Chat, as the [Body](../application/Body.md#body) says, from the ribbon's Planning tab or the palette's "Planning". Once opened, its tab stays: it has no close button, and nothing closes it. It is a kanban board, filling the body beneath the tab bar, as the [Body](../application/Body.md#body) places it, from the tab bar down to the bottom of the window and across the whole width of the window, from its left edge to its right, since neither the chat input nor either sidebar is shown while Planning's tab is selected. Its sections sit side by side as columns, in the order work moves, left to right: "Draft", the first, "Backlog", "Next sprint", "Current sprint", and "Done", then "Findings", the last, as research says. Draft holds rough stories, ideas written down quickly that aren't ready to plan yet, until they are moved to the Backlog. The Draft column is not drafting: "Draft stories", the Backlog's button that asks the agent for proposals, as creating says, is apart from it and never puts anything in it. Each column is headed by its name and count, with its actions, as the layout says, and holds its stories as cards, one beneath another. Every column is always there, whatever it holds; one with nothing in it shows its empty state, as the states say.
27:"Spec", "Ask", or "Freeform", in that mode's colour, as the [PromptModes](../prompt-mode/modes/index.md#prompt-modes) colours it; its step count and a small icon for anything attached, when it has them; and, once it has been sent, its status, as a task's is shown, at the row's right. Hovering a card shows a menu button at its top right. A story has no Send of its own, as the sprint says.
28:A column is too narrow to open a story in place, so clicking a card never expands it beneath itself or moves the cards below it: it opens the story in the side panel, docked at the board's right, as the layout places it and the parts draw a panel, headed by the story's title. The panel holds all an open story holds: its title, editable in place; its prompt, in a [GrowingTextInput](../../ui/components/growing-text-input/index.md#growing-text-input) with the chat input's highlighting, completions, and slicing, as the [ChatInput](../prompt-mode/chat-input/index.md#chat-input) has, and its attachments beneath; its parameters, its mode, model, effort, and Slice, as the chat input shows them; its steps, with Add step, as its steps say; and its Spec citations, as the spec grounding says. Every change is saved as it is made. While its story is open, its card stays highlighted, its line in the theme's accent, so which story the panel shows is always plain. Only one story is open at a time: clicking another card opens that one in the panel instead. Escape, the panel's close button, or clicking the open story's card again closes it, and its card's highlight with it.
35:A story can also be prompted into existence: "Draft stories", a button beside the Backlog's heading, and Draft Stories on the [RibbonPlanningTab](../../ui/components/ribbon/planning-tab/index.md#ribbon-planning-tab), which opens Planning first, open a small box to say what is wanted, in a sentence or a paragraph, whose request asks the harness, in a run of its own with no session, with the planning prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts), to break it into stories, each with a title, a prompt, and the mode that fits it. They come back as proposals, as the drafting and proposals say, each to be accepted, edited, or rejected, and none reaches the plan until it is accepted. An Ask answer's prompt cards also offer "Add to sprint", which adds the card's prompt as a story in its mode to Next sprint.
41:Each story starts a conversation of its own, as though New conversation had been pressed just before it, and so does each later step of a story's chain: a story never carries on the conversation of the story before it, nor of anything its lane ran before the sprint. New conversation, pressed before the sprint was sent, is left for the next prompt written in the chat input.
46:Close sprint treats a story whose task is missing as it treats one that failed or was cancelled: it goes back to the start of Next sprint, with the others sent again, in their order, never into Done. Closing saves the sections, Done, Next sprint, the Backlog, Draft, and the emptied Current sprint, to `order.json` straight away, as the [ProjectData](../project-data/index.md#project-data) says, and Current sprint's state is worked out afresh from the stories' tasks whenever the plan is read, so a sprint that finished, or whose tasks went missing, before a reload or a restart can still be closed after it, as before.
50:Dragging a card on the board feels as it does in Trello, and the board opens the gap, as the [DragReorderBehaviour](../../ui/behaviours/drag-reorder/index.md#drag-reorder-behaviour) lets its user do, in place of the accent line alone. Only the board's cards move this way; steps in an open story, and every other list, are dragged as the [DragReorderBehaviour](../../ui/behaviours/drag-reorder/index.md#drag-reorder-behaviour) says, with its line.
57:The open story's card is kept in view, however the board changes around it. As the side panel opens and the board narrows, the board scrolls sideways alongside it, in the same quick motion, just enough to keep the open story's card fully in view, when it was fully in view before; a card already partly out of view is left where it is. A story opened from anywhere outside the board, as a proposal's "Added to Backlog" or "Added to <story>" link, or a palette or ribbon action that opens one, scrolls the board sideways and its column up or down, in the same motion, just enough that its card is fully in view, then highlights it as the open story. Clicking a card only partly in view opens it and scrolls the board and its column just enough to show it all; a card already fully in view never scrolls anything. Each scroll moves only as far as it must, the card landing at the nearest edge of the view, never centred.
58:Nothing above the view moves what is in it. In a column scrolled away from its top, a card above the view that is added, deleted, or changes height, as a story's status or step lines arriving in Current sprint, or a sprint closing and its stories leaving, never moves the cards in view: the column's scroll shifts by the same amount, at once and with no motion, so they stay exactly where they were, and the column's thumb moves without jumping, as the [Scrollbar.steadiness](../../ui/components/scrollbar/index.md#steadiness) says. A column at its top keeps its top, and cards in view move as the motion above says.
63:Next sprint can start on its own: a "Start automatically" switch on Next sprint's heading, beside Send sprint, its tooltip saying the next sprint starts as soon as the current one is done. It starts off, and is saved with the plan, in `order.json`, so it is kept across reloads and restarts.
71:The stories, their order, and which section each is in are saved with the project's data, as the [ProjectData](../project-data/index.md#project-data) says, in `.suspense/planning/`, a file per story, holding its hidden anchor as a queued prompt's is, and an `order.json` listing the sections, Draft, the Backlog, Next sprint, Current sprint, and Done, and their stories in order, so the plan is kept with the project and comes back as it was left. A plan saved before Draft existed, its `order.json` with no Draft section, loads as it was with Draft empty, and is saved with one the next time the plan is. A story's steps are saved in its own file, in their order, as its steps say, never apart from it.
73:## Steps
75:A story can have steps: one or more further prompts, each a piece of the story done after the one before, as a chain's steps are. Each step has a prompt, a mode of its own, Code or Spec, starting as the story's, and a model and effort, starting as the story's, and may have attachments. A story with no steps is just its own prompt, as before; its prompt is always its first step.
76:An open story lists its steps beneath its prompt, in order, each a row numbered from 2, the story's own prompt being 1, with its mode pill and the first line of its prompt, opening in place to edit, as a story does. "Add step", beneath the last, adds one at the end, open and focused; several can be added one after another, Enter in an empty step's prompt adding none. Steps are reordered by dragging, as the [DragReorderBehaviour](../../ui/behaviours/drag-reorder/index.md#drag-reorder-behaviour) says, or Alt+Up and Alt+Down, and each has a remove button in its row and in its menu. The story's own prompt stays first and can't be moved among them.
77:A step belongs to its story and to nothing else. It is saved in the story's file, in a `steps` list of the story's hidden anchor, in its order, and saved as every change to the plan is, straight away; a story duplicated copies its steps, and one deleted deletes them. Its order is kept wherever the story goes, between sections, into a sprint, and back from Done.
78:Wherever the story is shown, its steps are too: its card in Planning shows how many beneath its title, muted, as "3 steps", with the list in its tooltip; the open story lists them as above; a proposal to add to it shows them, so what it would add to can be seen; and once it is sent, it is sent as a chain, its prompt first and each step after it in order, each step sent only once the one before it finishes, as the [PromptEditor](../prompt-mode/index.md#prompt-editor)'s chained tasks are, so the Tasks tab shows the story as one chain with its steps beneath it. A story sent can't be given more steps; its steps, sent or still to come, read as they were sent. In Current sprint, a story with steps lists its steps on its card, beneath its title, a line for each step, in order, each with its number, the first line of its prompt cut off with an ellipsis, and its own status, as a running chain's are in the Tasks tab; in Done, and in a finding started from the story, its steps are listed the same way, collapsed to its step count until it is expanded. The same steps, in the same order, are what every place shows, since every place reads them from the story's own file.
79:A step is only ever added to a story that exists. Adding one to a story that doesn't, as one deleted while a proposal naming it waited, or by another window, or whose file was removed from `.suspense/planning/` meanwhile, changes nothing: no story is made for it, no other story is given it, and nothing is saved. It says so in plain words where it was asked for, as "That story no longer exists, so the step wasn't added", in the theme's error colour, as the states say, with what was to be added kept, so it can be added to another story or as a new one. A step added to a story already sent is refused the same way, saying the story has been sent.
83:Everything in Planning is laid out on one rhythm, so the board scans at a glance. The board fills the body beneath the tab bar, with 24 pixels of padding at its top, sides, and foot. Its columns are all the same fixed width, 320 pixels, 16 pixels apart, each as tall as the board, set side by side from its left edge in the order work moves, Draft, the first, Backlog, Next sprint, Current sprint, Done, and Findings, the last. Each column is a list, as Trello draws one: a container of its own, as the parts draw a list, on the theme's recessed surface, one step from the base the board is on, its corners rounded 8 pixels, with its heading and its cards inside it, and no border. Three things set a list apart from the board's base, all quieter than any card's state: its recessed surface; the theme's pressed bevel around it, inside its rounded corners, so it reads as sunk into the base; and the 16 pixel gaps between columns. Each column also has a look of its own, telling it from its neighbours: a 2 pixel edge along the list's top, inside its rounded corners and above its heading, in the colour its status icon uses, as the parts give it, laid over the recessed surface at 50%, so Draft's is tertiary text, as the Backlog's is, the two told apart by their icons, Next sprint's secondary text, Current sprint's the accent, Done's success, and Findings' secondary text. The edge is the only place a column's colour shows besides its icon: its heading, its name, its cards, its empty state, and the drag placeholder keep their own surfaces and colours untouched, the placeholder its well surface. Laid over at half strength and 2 pixels tall, a column's edge is always quieter than a card's hover, its selected surface, its focus ring, or the open story's 3 pixel accent line, as the Theme's hierarchy orders decoration below state.
84:Each column's heading reads as Linear heads a group: a row 32 pixels tall at the list's top, holding, from its left, the column's status icon, 14 pixels, as the parts give it; its name in small semibold text, in the theme's primary text colour; its count beside it, muted; and its actions at its right, each a labelled button, never an icon alone, as the sections give them: "Draft stories" and "Research" on the Backlog; "Send sprint" and "Start automatically" on Next sprint; "Close sprint" on Current sprint, once its sprint can close. A heading with more actions than fit its width wraps them onto a second line beneath its name rather than cutting any off; Draft's heading has no actions but its "+ Add". On Draft, the Backlog, and Next sprint, hovering the heading, or focus anywhere in it, shows "+ Add" just after its count, a small ghost button with a plus icon and its label, never the icon alone, its tooltip saying it adds a story at the top of the list; it takes its room in the heading whether shown or not, so the heading's actions never shift as it appears, and the other columns' headings have none. 8 pixels beneath the heading come its cards, one beneath another, 8 pixels apart, each the list's full inner width.
88:The side panel is the one place anything opens: an open story, Draft stories, and Research, never beneath a heading or a card, and never in a popup or over the board. A composer is not an opened story but a card being written, and is the one thing drawn in a list, where its story will sit. It is docked at the right of Planning's tab, 400 pixels wide, as tall as the tab, from the tab bar down to the bottom of the body, a 1 pixel line in the theme's line colour along its left edge, and it scrolls up and down on its own, its heading staying at its top, with the [Scrollbar](../../ui/components/scrollbar/index.md#scrollbar) along its right while it holds more than fits. Only one thing is in it at a time: opening a story, drafting, or research replaces whatever it held, and closing it leaves nothing there. While it is open the board narrows to the space left of it, its columns keeping their width and scrolling sideways as ever when they no longer fit; the panel never covers the board, and the board never covers it. Closed, it takes no space and the board fills the tab again. It slides in and out at the right, as motion says.
89:While paused, the pause bar runs across the top of the whole board, above every column, as the paused section says, beside the side panel when it is open, never over it. Each column is headed as above, so nothing sits outside the rhythm, and an open story, a drafting or research panel, and the citations and steps inside a story all use the same five parts, below.

100:Stories are selected as Linear selects issues, to be acted on together. Only a story in Draft, the Backlog, or Next sprint can be selected, the three lists that take moves, as the sprint says; a card in Current sprint or Done, and a finding, never is. Ctrl+click (Cmd+click on macOS) on a card toggles it alone in or out of the selection, the rest kept; Shift+click selects the range within its column from the card last clicked or toggled there to the clicked one, inclusive, added to the selection, or the clicked card alone when nothing in that column was clicked or toggled yet; and X toggles one from the keyboard, as the keyboard says. A selection can hold stories from all three lists at once. A plain click opens the story in the side panel as ever, and leaves the selection as it is. Selected cards show the theme's selected surface, as the story card above says, distinct from focus and from the open story's line. The selection is the board's own, never saved with the plan: a selected story leaves it when it is deleted or leaves Draft, the Backlog, and Next sprint, as when the sprint it is in is sent.
101:While any card is selected, the bulk bar runs across the board's foot, beneath its columns, beside the side panel when it is open, never over it nor over the board: a row 40 pixels tall, on the theme's raised surface with a 1 pixel line in the theme's line colour along its top, the columns shortening to make room as it slides up in motion's quick motion, and lengthening again as it slides away once nothing is selected. At its left it names how many are selected, as "3 selected"; at its right, labelled buttons, never icons alone, in this order: "Move to next sprint", which moves the selected stories in the Backlog to the end of Next sprint; "Move to backlog", which moves those in Draft and in Next sprint to the end of the Backlog; "Move to draft", which moves those in the Backlog to the end of Draft; "Draft with agent", which drafts each selected story in a run of its own, all at once, as drafting with the agent says; "Change mode", which opens a mode picker above it, the one a card's pill opens, setting the mode of every selected story to the one picked, their steps keeping their own; "Duplicate", which duplicates each, every copy just beneath its original, as a card's menu does; "Delete", which deletes them all after one confirmation naming how many, as "Delete 3 stories?"; and "Clear", which empties the selection. Every move keeps the stories' order, as the selection orders them, Draft's first, top to bottom, then the Backlog's, then Next sprint's, and animates as motion says; a move button is disabled, its tooltip saying why, while none of the selection is in the list it moves from. Moved stories stay selected, duplicates are not selected, and the originals stay so. The bar has no Send and sends nothing: a story still reaches work only through the sprint, as the sprint says.
124:A draft about a story, from "Ask agent" or "Draft with agent", may propose rewriting it: a proposal whose target is the story itself, to be rewritten, its title, prompt, mode, and spec citations replaced by the proposal's, its steps and attachments kept; as well as additions to it, each a step, as the proposals say. Where a story holds more than one piece of work, the draft splits it: the story's rewrite comes first, the first piece, with its steps, and each other piece is a proposal for a new story of its own, its target "New story", with its own title, prompt, mode, and spec citations, grounded as the spec grounding says. A story that is one piece of work gets only its rewrite and its steps, and no new story.
130:Each story drafts as "Ask agent" drafts an open story, with no request box and nothing to type: the run is told the story's title, its prompt, its mode, and its steps, and asked to turn it into a full story, a fuller prompt, the mode that fits it, the steps it needs, and its spec citations, grounded as the spec grounding says; and, where the story holds more than one piece of work, to split it, as drafting says: the story's rewrite, the first piece, with its steps, then a "New story" proposal for each other piece, each with its own title, prompt, mode, and spec citations, grounded the same way. A story that is one piece of work gets only its rewrite and its steps. Each run is its own, in a run of its own with no session, a Question run as the proposals say, with the planning prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts). All the runs start at once, none waiting on another, however many stories are drafted; one ending, failing, or being stopped never touches the others. A story already drafting is skipped, so drafting it again starts no second run.
133:A story's waiting proposals are kept until each is decided, saved with the plan as soon as its run ends, in `.suspense/planning/proposals/`, a file per story named by its id, so they come back, and its card's count with them, after closing Planning, a reload, or a restart. A story deleted takes its waiting proposals with it, and one duplicated copies none.
134:Nothing reaches the plan until it is accepted, as the proposals say. Accepting the story's rewrite rewrites it as proposed, and accepting an addition adds its step to the story's end, as the proposal cards say. Accepting a new story split off it adds that story at the end of the Backlog, marked "From agent", as the proposal cards say, never into Draft and never into Next sprint. A story in Draft whose rewrite or addition is accepted moves to the end of the Backlog, as "Move to backlog" moves it and motion animates it, staying open if it was; accepting a new story split off it never moves it, and a story in the Backlog or Next sprint stays where it is. A story sent while its proposals waited can no longer be rewritten or added to, as the proposal cards say.
159:The citations are shown on the proposal's card, beneath its prompt, as a short list, each element's name, as `Planning.sprint`, and its file, muted, as a link that opens that place in the Piton source, a "No match" entry muted with a small marker, and are kept with the story once it is accepted, listed in the open story beneath its steps as "Spec", and shown in its card's tooltip. They can be edited with the story, removed, or added to by hand: "Add citation" opens a picker of the spec's elements, its anchors and their properties, found from the Piton source and searched by name, never from the compiled reference, and adds the one picked in the same form, checked as a proposal's are.
160:Each citation says what kind of element it is, "Requirement", "Behaviour", "Name", or "Section", shown as a small muted label before it, and quotes the few words of the spec it rests on, so what the story relies on can be checked at a glance. Grounding holds for every proposal, however it came: from Draft stories, from research that ends with proposals, and from an Ask answer's `suspense-story` block, each checked and flagged the same way when it arrives.
165:Accept records it in the plan straight away, saved as any change to the plan is: a new story goes into the Backlog, at its end, in its mode, marked "From agent" with a small sparkle beside its title until it is first opened, its spec citations kept with it, whether it was accepted on its own card or with "Accept all", and whether it came from drafting, research, or an Ask answer. Accepting never adds anything to Next sprint, so it never starts a sprint, however Start automatically is set; an accepted story reaches a sprint only once it is moved to Next sprint, as the sprint says; an addition is added to the named story as a new step at its end, as its steps say, its attachments with it, and the story is marked "Updated by agent" until it is next opened, the new step shown highlighted the first time it is; where the story no longer exists by then, the step isn't added, as the steps say, and the card stays, its target changed to "New story", to be accepted again; a rewrite replaces the story's title, prompt, mode, and spec citations with the proposal's, keeping its steps and attachments, and marks it "Updated by agent" the same way, and where the story no longer exists by then, it is changed to "New story" the same way. A story already sent can't be added to or rewritten: its proposals offer only "New story". Each card, once decided, goes from the panel, and an accepted one leaves a one-line muted note in its place, "Added to Backlog" or "Added to <story>", with a link that opens the story where it is, in the Backlog or its section.
170:The agent can be asked to research a topic before anything is planned or built: "Research", a button beside "Draft stories" on the Backlog's heading, Research on the [RibbonPlanningTab](../../ui/components/ribbon/planning-tab/index.md#ribbon-planning-tab), which opens Planning first, and "Research" in an open story's menu, open a box labelled "What should be looked into?". Research runs as a read-only investigation, a Question run, as the [Sandbox](../sandbox/index.md#sandbox) says, which can read the project and the web and change nothing, given the research prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts), which asks for findings, not changes: what was found, where, with links to the files and lines it read, what it is unsure of, and what it would suggest, and it may end with proposals, as the proposals say. Its box and its run are shown in the side panel, replacing whatever it held, as drafting's are, its live line of what it is reading, a Stop button, and its spinner, and its proposals in the same cards with the same buttons as drafting's, with Accept all and Reject all at its foot.
171:Its findings are a document of their own, "Research: <topic>", rendered as markdown with its links clickable, shown in the Findings column, the board's last, after Done, newest first, each a row with its topic, when it ran, and its state, and opening in a body tab of its own to read whole. Findings started from a story are also listed in that story, beneath its steps. They are saved with the plan, in `.suspense/planning/research/`, a markdown file each, so they can be read again later, and can be deleted from their row's menu.
176:Nothing of the plan starts while it is paused, not only nothing new: a story of Current sprint still waiting in the queue, not yet started, is held where it is, as a queued prompt being edited is held, as the [QueueList](../prompt-mode/queue-list/index.md#queue-list) says, its card reading "Held for review", muted, and its lane waits on it; a story already running when the pause began runs on to its end, since stopping it would leave its work half done, and the bar says how many are still running. A chain's next step, as a story's steps are sent, is held the same way. Once the pause ends, by Resume or Approve, what was held goes on in its order, as though nothing had stopped it.
178:The pause is saved with the plan, in `order.json`, with the research it waits on, as soon as it begins, so it is still paused, and still shown so, after the project is closed and opened again, the application restarted, or the window reloaded, until it is resumed or approved.
182:Everything in Planning can be done from the keyboard. Tab moves through the columns' heading buttons, "+ Add" among them on Draft, the Backlog, and Next sprint, the cards and rows, each list's "Add story" line after its last card, and the open story's fields in order, and the focused one shows the theme's focus ring. Alt+Up and Alt+Down move the focused story up or down within its column, and Alt+Left and Alt+Right move it into the column beside it, as dragging does, where that column takes it: from Draft Alt+Right moves it to the end of the Backlog; from the Backlog Alt+Left moves it to the end of Draft and Alt+Right to the end of Next sprint; and from Next sprint Alt+Left moves it to the end of the Backlog, focus going with it; Alt+Left in Draft, the first column, does nothing; anywhere else, as a story in Current sprint or Done, or toward a column that takes no drops, they do nothing. Ctrl+N (Cmd+N on macOS) opens the composer at the foot of the list that has focus, Draft, the Backlog, or Next sprint, when focus is on its heading, one of its cards, or its "Add story" line, and at the foot of Next sprint otherwise, as when focus is in Current sprint, Done, Findings, the side panel, or nowhere on the board, scrolling the board sideways to show it. In a composer, Enter adds the story and opens the next composer beneath it, focus moving to its title, and Escape discards it, focus going back to what opened it, the "Add story" line or the heading's "+ Add". Ctrl+Enter moves the focused story to Next sprint, or to the Backlog from it or from Draft, Ctrl+Shift+Enter sends the sprint, and M opens the focused story's mode picker, whose options are picked with the arrow keys and Enter. Delete deletes the focused story after a confirmation. X toggles the focused story in or out of the selection, as the parts say. While no card has focus, the shortcuts that act on one story, Enter, X, M, Ctrl+Enter, Delete, and the Alt arrows, act on the card under the pointer instead, as Linear's do, without moving focus to it, and do nothing when the pointer is over no card; with a card focused, they act on it, wherever the pointer is. None of them sends a story, as the sprint says. Escape closes what is open, a composer, a menu, the selection, cleared before anything else closes, then the side panel, whether it holds a story, drafting, or research, one at a time.
