120:## Drafting
122:Drafting stories shows its work in one place, the side panel, docked at the board's right, as the layout places it, replacing whatever it held, never in a popup that hides the list. 123:Drafting can also be started from an open story, with "Ask agent" in its card's menu or the open story's footer: the request box then reads "What does this story need?", the run is told the story's title and prompt, and the drafting panel takes the open story's place in the side panel, the story's card staying highlighted while the draft is about it.
124:A draft about a story, from "Ask agent" or "Draft with agent", never changes the story it drafts: every piece it proposes is a new story of its own, its target "New story", and the drafted story keeps its title, prompt, mode, steps, attachments, and spec citations, and stays in its column. A story that is one piece of work gets only that first new story. A draft about a story proposes nothing that rewrites the story or adds to it, none of its proposals targeting the story drafted, and a follow-up step is always part of the new story it follows, never a proposal of its own.
125:A draft favours several stories over one story of many steps. Each story it proposes is one piece of work a single chain does: as a rule a Chain story, its prompt changing the spec and then the code to match it, with at most one step, a Spec step following the chain up, where the spec needs settling once the code is done. Work that would need more than that is split into stories of their own, each a "New story" proposal, never strung onto one story as further steps. A story drafted that already has steps keeps them, since the draft never changes it; each new story proposed carries no more than the one Spec follow-up, and what further steps would have done is proposed as new stories of its own.

143:## Proposals
146:Each proposal is one concrete task, with a title, a prompt, and the mode that fits it, and says where it should go: either "New story", to become a story of its own, or "Add to" a story, naming an existing story the run was shown, to be added to it. A draft about a story, from "Ask agent" or "Draft with agent", proposes only new stories, as drafting says, never one that adds to or rewrites the story drafted. The planning prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts) asks for each proposal as a fenced block whose info string is `suspense-story`, holding its title, its mode, its target, `new` or the story's id, its `spec` list of citations into the Piton source, as the spec grounding says, and its prompt, so the application reads it whatever else the reply says. A new story's Spec follow-up step, in a draft about a story, is a block of its own just after the story it follows, its target `step`, holding the step's mode, Spec, and its prompt, and is read as that story's step, part of its proposal and never a proposal apart. The planning prompt explains the form with an example, as:

158:A block that names a story that no longer exists is shown as "New story", with a muted note saying so. In a draft about a story, a block whose target is the story drafted, by its id or as `rewrite`, as one saved before drafts proposed only new stories, is read as `new`, and its card reads "New story"; a block that would add a step to the story drafted is read as the follow-up step of the new story just before it, or, where there is none, as a new story of its own.
163:Each proposal cites what it found, specifically: its `suspense-story` block holds a `spec` list, one entry per element, each a link to the element in the spec's Piton source, under the spec location, never to the compiled reference: the .pi file, relative to the project directory, then `#` and the element it names, an anchor or a property on one, in the form `piton slice` takes, as `spec/scope/planning/index.pi#Planning.sprint`, with a short line of what in it the story touches. The story's prompt names those elements and links to them in the same form. Only elements the drafter found are cited, and where they point is the only thing that is Piton source: the drafter still learns what the spec says only from the compiled reference or from what `piton slice` prints, never by reading the Piton source, as the [ModeSystemPrompts](../prompt-mode/mode-system-prompts/index.md#mode-system-prompts) says, and works out each element's source from what `piton slice` prints for it, which names the file each declaration is in.
164:The application checks each link against the Piton source when the proposal arrives: the file has to exist under the spec location, and has to define the named element, the anchor declared in it and, for a property, that property on it, as `piton slice` would find it. A link that fails either check is dropped, with a muted note on the card saying which link it dropped and why, "no such file" or "the file doesn't define Planning.sprint". Each citation, on the proposal card and in an accepted story's "Spec" list, opens that place in the Piton source, the file in the editor at the element's declaration, as the [OpenFile](../open-file/index.md#open-file) opens a file.
165:Where no spec element matches part of a story, as a request for something the spec doesn't describe yet, the story says so instead of inventing one: its `spec` list holds an entry reading "No match", with what was looked for, and its prompt says plainly that the spec has nothing on it yet, so the change is new. A proposal with no element at all, and no "No match", isn't shown as a story: its card says the drafter cited no spec, in the theme's warning colour, with "Draft again", which runs the draft for that story once more.
166:The citations are shown on the proposal's card, beneath its prompt, as a short list, each element's name, as `Planning.sprint`, and its file, muted, as a link that opens that place in the Piton source, a "No match" entry muted with a small marker, and are kept with the story once it is accepted, listed in the open story beneath its steps as "Spec", and shown in its card's tooltip. They can be edited with the story, removed, or added to by hand: "Add citation" opens a picker of the spec's elements, its anchors and their properties, found from the Piton source and searched by name, never from the compiled reference, and adds the one picked in the same form, checked as a proposal's are.
167:Each citation says what kind of element it is, "Requirement", "Behaviour", "Name", or "Section", shown as a small muted label before it, and quotes the few words of the spec it rests on, so what the story relies on can be checked at a glance. Grounding holds for every proposal, however it came: from Draft stories, from research that ends with proposals, and from an Ask answer's `suspense-story` block, each checked and flagged the same way when it arrives.
