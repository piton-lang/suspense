## Steps

A story can have steps: one or more further prompts, each a piece of the story done after the one before, as a chain's steps are. Each step has a prompt, a mode of its own, Code or Spec, starting as the story's, and a model and effort, starting as the story's, and may have attachments. A story with no steps is just its own prompt, as before; its prompt is always its first step.
An open story lists its steps beneath its prompt, in order, each a row numbered from 2, the story's own prompt being 1, with its mode pill and the first line of its prompt, opening in place to edit, as a story does. "Add step", beneath the last, adds one at the end, open and focused; several can be added one after another, Enter in an empty step's prompt adding none. Steps are reordered by dragging, as the [DragReorderBehaviour](../../ui/behaviours/drag-reorder/index.md#drag-reorder-behaviour) says, or Alt+Up and Alt+Down, and each has a remove button in its row and in its menu. The story's own prompt stays first and can't be moved among them.
A step belongs to its story and to nothing else. It is saved in the story's file, in a `steps` list of the story's hidden anchor, in its order, and saved as every change to the plan is, straight away; a story duplicated copies its steps, and one deleted deletes them. Its order is kept wherever the story goes, between sections, into a sprint, and back from Done.
Wherever the story is shown, its steps are too: its card in Planning shows how many beneath its title, muted, as "3 steps", with the list in its tooltip; the open story lists them as above; a proposal to add to it shows them, so what it would add to can be seen; and once it is sent, it is sent as a chain, its prompt first and each step after it in order, each step sent only once the one before it finishes, as the [PromptEditor](../prompt-mode/index.md#prompt-editor)'s chained tasks are, so the Tasks tab shows the story as one chain with its steps beneath it. A story sent can't be given more steps; its steps, sent or still to come, read as they were sent. In Current sprint, a story with steps lists its steps on its card, beneath its title, a line for each step, in order, each with its number, the first line of its prompt cut off with an ellipsis, and its own status, as a running chain's are in the Tasks tab; in Done, and in a finding started from the story, its steps are listed the same way, collapsed to its step count until it is expanded. The same steps, in the same order, are what every place shows, since every place reads them from the story's own file.
A step is only ever added to a story that exists. Adding one to a story that doesn't, as one deleted while a proposal naming it waited, or by another window, or whose file was removed from `.suspense/planning/` meanwhile, changes nothing: no story is made for it, no other story is given it, and nothing is saved. It says so in plain words where it was asked for, as "That story no longer exists, so the step wasn't added", in the theme's error colour, as the states say, with what was to be added kept, so it can be added to another story or as a new one. A step added to a story already sent is refused the same way, saying the story has been sent.

## Layout

Everything in Planning is laid out on one rhythm, so the board scans at a glance. The board fills the body beneath the tab bar, with 24 pixels of padding at its top, sides, and foot. Its columns are all the same fixed width, 320 pixels, 16 pixels apart, each as tall as the board, set side by side from its left edge in the order work moves, Draft, the first, Backlog, Next sprint, Current sprint, Done, and Findings, the last. Each column is a list, as Trello draws one: a container of its own, as the parts draw a list, on the theme's recessed surface, one step from the base the board is on, its corners rounded 8 pixels, with its heading and its cards inside it, and no border. Three things set a list apart from the board's base, all quieter than any card's state: its recessed surface; the theme's pressed bevel around it, inside its rounded corners, so it reads as sunk into the base; and the 16 pixel gaps between columns. Each column also has a look of its own, telling it from its neighbours: a 2 pixel edge along the list's top, inside its rounded corners and above its heading, in the colour its status icon uses, as the parts give it, laid over the recessed surface at 50%, so Draft's is tertiary text, as the Backlog's is, the two told apart by their icons, Next sprint's secondary text, Current sprint's the accent, Done's success, and Findings' secondary text. The edge is the only place a column's colour shows besides its icon: its heading, its name, its cards, its empty state, and the drag placeholder keep their own surfaces and colours untouched, the placeholder its well surface. Laid over at half strength and 2 pixels tall, a column's edge is always quieter than a card's hover, its selected surface, its focus ring, or the open story's 3 pixel accent line, as the Theme's hierarchy orders decoration below state.
Each column's heading reads as Linear heads a group: a row 32 pixels tall at the list's top, holding, from its left, the column's status icon, 14 pixels, as the parts give it; its name in small semibold text, in the theme's primary text colour; its count beside it, muted; and its actions at its right, each a labelled button, never an icon alone, as the sections give them: "Draft stories" and "Research" on the Backlog; "Send sprint" and "Start automatically" on Next sprint; "Close sprint" on Current sprint, once its sprint can close. A heading with more actions than fit its width wraps them onto a second line beneath its name rather than cutting any off; Draft's heading has no actions but its "+ Add". On Draft, the Backlog, and Next sprint, hovering the heading, or focus anywhere in it, shows "+ Add" just after its count, a small ghost button with a plus icon and its label, never the icon alone, its tooltip saying it adds a story at the top of the list; it takes its room in the heading whether shown or not, so the heading's actions never shift as it appears, and the other columns' headings have none. 8 pixels beneath the heading come its cards, one beneath another, 8 pixels apart, each the list's full inner width.
At the foot of Draft, of the Backlog, and of Next sprint, 8 pixels beneath its last card, or beneath its empty state, is its "Add story" line: a row 32 pixels tall, the list's full inner width, with a plus icon and the label "Add story" in small muted text, its corners rounded 6 pixels, and no border; hovered, it takes the theme's hover surface and its text the theme's primary text colour. It scrolls with the cards, after the last of them. Its composer, as creating says, is drawn where it was clicked, at the foot in the line's place, or at the top, above the first card, from "+ Add": a story card, as the parts draw one, its title a text box in the card's title text, with the focus ring around the card, and beneath it, muted, "Enter to add, Escape to cancel"; the cards below it move down to make room, and the list scrolls to keep it in view. Current sprint, Done, and Findings have no "Add story" line.
Each column scrolls up and down on its own, its heading staying at its top while its cards scroll beneath it, inside the list's rounded corners, with the [Scrollbar](../../ui/components/scrollbar/index.md#scrollbar) shown along the list's right, inside it, while it holds more than fits. The board scrolls sideways when its columns are wider than the tab, with the [Scrollbar](../../ui/components/scrollbar/index.md#scrollbar) along its foot; while they fit, it doesn't scroll. The wheel and the trackpad scroll what is under the pointer, each part of the board on its own. A vertical wheel turn or two-finger swipe over a column, its heading included, scrolls only that column up or down; it never scrolls the board sideways, and it never passes on to the board, not when the column is at its top or its end, nor when it has nothing to scroll. Shift with the wheel, a sideways trackpad swipe, or a mouse's horizontal wheel, tilted or a thumb wheel, anywhere on the board, over a column, its heading, or the padding and gaps around them, scrolls the board sideways while its columns are wider than the tab, and does nothing while they fit. The horizontal wheel never scrolls a column up or down, and it moves the board the way it is turned, right moving the board right and left moving it left. A vertical wheel turn over the board outside every column, on its padding, in the 16 pixel gaps between columns, or in the space beside the last column, scrolls the board sideways instead, as Trello does, down moving it right and up left. A diagonal trackpad swipe over a column scrolls the column up or down and the board sideways together, each by its own share of the swipe, the up and down share to the column and the sideways share to the board. Scrolling over the side panel, the pause bar, or the bulk bar never moves the board or any of its columns: the side panel scrolls only itself, and the two bars scroll nothing. Every card's state reads the same way, as below, and every label is the same small text, muted where it is secondary.
The board is kept as it was left while Planning's tab stays open, as the [Body](../application/Body.md#body) keeps a tab. Selecting another tab and then Planning's again shows the board scrolled sideways exactly where it was left, and every column scrolled up or down exactly where it was, at once, with nothing animating, sliding, or scrolling into view, as though the tab had never been left; nothing is scrolled to its start or to a card on the way back. The side panel opening or closing in the meantime, as a story opened from outside the board, changes only the board's sideways scroll, as far as the panel's opening or closing needs, as motion says, and shows it already settled when the tab is back; every column keeps its own scroll through it. The scroll is the board's own, kept only while its tab is open, and never saved with the plan, in `order.json` or anywhere else: closing Planning's tab and opening it again, or reloading the window, starts the board at its left edge and every column at its top.
The side panel is the one place anything opens: an open story, Draft stories, and Research, never beneath a heading or a card, and never in a popup or over the board. A composer is not an opened story but a card being written, and is the one thing drawn in a list, where its story will sit. It is docked at the right of Planning's tab, 400 pixels wide, as tall as the tab, from the tab bar down to the bottom of the body, a 1 pixel line in the theme's line colour along its left edge, and it scrolls up and down on its own, its heading staying at its top, with the [Scrollbar](../../ui/components/scrollbar/index.md#scrollbar) along its right while it holds more than fits. Only one thing is in it at a time: opening a story, drafting, or research replaces whatever it held, and closing it leaves nothing there. While it is open the board narrows to the space left of it, its columns keeping their width and scrolling sideways as ever when they no longer fit; the panel never covers the board, and the board never covers it. Closed, it takes no space and the board fills the tab again. It slides in and out at the right, as motion says.
While paused, the pause bar runs across the top of the whole board, above every column, as the paused section says, beside the side panel when it is open, never over it. Each column is headed as above, so nothing sits outside the rhythm, and an open story, a drafting or research panel, and the citations and steps inside a story all use the same five parts, below.

## Parts

Everything in Planning is built from five parts, each always drawn the same way, in the theme's roles, named as the theme names them, in both dark and light mode:
- A list: one per column, as the layout places it, 320 pixels wide and as tall as the board, on the theme's recessed surface, one step from the base, with no border, the theme's pressed bevel inside its edges, its corners rounded 8 pixels, 8 pixels of padding; a 2 pixel edge along its top, in its status icon's colour laid over the recessed surface at 50%, as the layout says, the column's one look of its own; its heading row at its top, as the layout says, then its cards, or its empty state centred beneath its heading; its heading's status icon one per column, each always the same: Draft a pencil in the theme's tertiary text colour, a drawn glyph and never a circle, so it can't be mistaken for the Backlog's; the Backlog a dashed circle in the theme's tertiary text colour; Next sprint an empty circle in the theme's secondary text colour; Current sprint a half-filled circle in the theme's accent; Done a filled circle with a check in the theme's success colour; and Findings a magnifying glass in the theme's secondary text colour; each, with the list's top edge in its colour, telling its column apart at a glance while still only repeating the column's name, never standing for it alone
- A story card: a box the full inner width of its list, on the theme's raised surface, with a 1 pixel line in the theme's line colour, corners rounded 6 pixels, 8 pixels of padding above and below and 10 at its sides, 8 pixels from the next; a top line with its drag grip, 16 pixels, in the theme's tertiary text colour, at its left, its title beside it, the card's primary text, in up to two lines, cut off with an ellipsis after the second, and its menu button at its right on hover and always while it is focused; beneath the title, 4 pixels below it, a single property row, 20 pixels tall, in small muted text, of small items 8 pixels apart: its mode pill, in its mode's colour, as the PromptModes colours it, its step count, its attachment icon, each when it has them, and its status and time, right-aligned, once it has been sent; and, in Current sprint, its steps' lines beneath, as its steps say; used for every story, in every column, muted in Done. Its states are three distinct steps, as the Theme's hierarchy orders them: hovering raises it one surface step, to the theme's hover surface; the open story keeps a 3 pixel line in the theme's accent along its left edge, as the stories say, whether or not it is hovered; a selected card, as the selection below says, takes the theme's selected surface in place of its raised one, hovered or not; and focus shows the theme's focus ring, in the accent, 2 pixels around it, over any of them, so a hover is always quieter than a selection, and a selection than focus, as the Theme orders them, and the open story's line, an edge rather than a surface, reads apart from both, a card being open, selected, and focused at once without any of the three hiding another
- A row: 36 pixels tall, for steps in an open story and for findings; its secondary text muted and cut off with an ellipsis, its buttons small ghost buttons with labels, shown at its right on hover and always while it is focused
- A panel: the side panel, docked at the board's right, full height, as the layout places it, on the theme's raised surface, its corners square, 12 pixels of padding, a heading row of 32 pixels with its name, as "Draft stories", "Research", or the story's title, and its close button, its content beneath, and its actions in a footer row, right-aligned, the primary action last; used for an open story, drafting, and research
- A card: a box inside a panel, on the theme's base surface, with a 1 pixel line in the theme's line colour, corners rounded 4 pixels, 12 pixels of padding, 8 pixels from the next; a label row at its top, its content, and a footer row of labelled buttons; used for proposals and for each finding's summary
Labels are words, never icons alone: every button reads what it does, "Accept", "Send sprint", "Start automatically", and an icon beside a label only repeats it. Each heading's count is a plain number, muted, and each status a coloured label, as the states say. The pause bar is a row across the whole board's top, above its columns, 40 pixels tall, its text and buttons on one line, the same height whatever it says.
Stories are selected as Linear selects issues, to be acted on together. Only a story in Draft, the Backlog, or Next sprint can be selected, the three lists that take moves, as the sprint says; a card in Current sprint or Done, and a finding, never is. Ctrl+click (Cmd+click on macOS) on a card toggles it alone in or out of the selection, the rest kept; Shift+click selects the range within its column from the card last clicked or toggled there to the clicked one, inclusive, added to the selection, or the clicked card alone when nothing in that column was clicked or toggled yet; and X toggles one from the keyboard, as the keyboard says. A selection can hold stories from all three lists at once. A plain click opens the story in the side panel as ever, and leaves the selection as it is. Selected cards show the theme's selected surface, as the story card above says, distinct from focus and from the open story's line. The selection is the board's own, never saved with the plan: a selected story leaves it when it is deleted or leaves Draft, the Backlog, and Next sprint, as when the sprint it is in is sent.
While any card is selected, the bulk bar runs across the board's foot, beneath its columns, beside the side panel when it is open, never over it nor over the board: a row 40 pixels tall, on the theme's raised surface with a 1 pixel line in the theme's line colour along its top, the columns shortening to make room as it slides up in motion's quick motion, and lengthening again as it slides away once nothing is selected. At its left it names how many are selected, as "3 selected"; at its right, labelled buttons, never icons alone, in this order: "Move to next sprint", which moves the selected stories in the Backlog to the end of Next sprint; "Move to backlog", which moves those in Draft and in Next sprint to the end of the Backlog; "Move to draft", which moves those in the Backlog to the end of Draft; "Draft with agent", which drafts each selected story in a run of its own, all at once, as drafting with the agent says; "Change mode", which opens a mode picker above it, the one a card's pill opens, setting the mode of every selected story to the one picked, their steps keeping their own; "Duplicate", which duplicates each, every copy just beneath its original, as a card's menu does; "Delete", which deletes them all after one confirmation naming how many, as "Delete 3 stories?"; and "Clear", which empties the selection. Every move keeps the stories' order, as the selection orders them, Draft's first, top to bottom, then the Backlog's, then Next sprint's, and animates as motion says; a move button is disabled, its tooltip saying why, while none of the selection is in the list it moves from. Moved stories stay selected, duplicates are not selected, and the originals stay so. The bar has no Send and sends nothing: a story still reaches work only through the sprint, as the sprint says.
Dragging a selected card by its grip carries the whole selection, in its order: the other selected cards leave their places as it lifts, their columns closing up, and the lifted copy is the grabbed card, as motion draws it, with the cards it carries stacked a few pixels behind it and their count beside its title. The gap opens as tall as the cards together, 8 pixels apart, and dropped there they settle into it in their order, all in that list, saved at once; dropped anywhere else, or cancelled with Escape, they all settle back where they were. Dragging a card that isn't selected moves it alone and leaves the selection as it is.
Right-clicking a card opens its menu at the pointer, the same menu its hover button opens, with the same items, acting on that card alone, in place of the browser's own; it closes as that menu does, Escape among the ways. The one exception is "Draft with agent": right-clicking a selected card while more than one card is selected gives it the selection, reading "Draft 3 with agent", and it drafts every selected story, as the bulk bar's button does; every other item still acts on that card alone.

## States

Each part of Planning has the same few states, shown the same way wherever they come up, so a state is never guessed from what is missing.
- Empty: a single muted line, centred in the column beneath its heading, saying what is missing and what to do, as "Nothing planned. Drag stories here, or press Ctrl+N.", with no illustration
- Loading: a spinner and a short muted label of what is happening, as "Drafting stories…", in the place the result will appear, never a blank space
- Paused: a story whose task is paused shows "Paused" as its status, in the theme's warning colour, with a small pause icon, and a "Resume" button on its card, as the PromptSending says of pausing; a sprint with any story paused says so in its heading, as "1 paused", in the same colour
- Error: a line in the theme's error colour, saying what went wrong in plain words, with what the harness or the application reported beneath it collapsed under "Details", and a "Try again" button where trying again can help
A story's status on its card is the same coloured label a task has, as the [MessageList](../prompt-mode/message-list/index.md#message-list)'s status says, Queued, Running with its spinner and time, Done, Failed, Cancelled, Paused, or, where its task can't be found, Task missing, as the sprint says, and a story not yet sent shows nothing there, so its absence means "not sent".
Each part shows each state that can come up for it, worded for it:
- Story list: empty, "Nothing planned. Press Ctrl+N to add a story." in Next sprint, "No drafts. Jot an idea down here." in Draft, "Nothing waiting." in the Backlog, "No sprint under way. Send sprint to start one." in Current sprint, and "Nothing done yet." in Done; loading, while the plan is read on opening, a few skeleton cards in each column, never an empty column; error, when the plan can't be read or saved, saying which file and why, with "Try again"
- Research and its findings: loading, the live line of what it is reading with its spinner; empty, "No findings yet." in Findings; error, the run's failure with "Try again", which keeps the question in its box; a finding that ended early, as stopped, marked "Incomplete", muted, in its row
- Pause: the pause bar, in the theme's warning colour, as the paused section says, and the sprint's Send buttons disabled with their tooltips saying why; a sprint that can't start for a failed story says so in its heading, in the same colour
- A story drafting with the agent: loading, "Drafting…" with its spinner in its card's property row; error, the run's failure on its card with "Details" and "Try again"; done, its waiting proposals counted on its card, as "3 proposals", or "Nothing proposed", muted, as drafting with the agent says
- Proposals: loading, the drafting line and spinner; empty, "No stories proposed." with "Draft again"; error, the run's failure with "Try again"; a proposal with no spec cited, in the warning colour with "Draft again", as the spec grounding says

## Drafting

Drafting stories shows its work in one place, the side panel, docked at the board's right, as the layout places it, replacing whatever it held, never in a popup that hides the list. It has the request box at its top, labelled "Describe what you want", and beneath it, while the run goes, its research: a muted line of what the harness is doing, as "Reading the spec…", with its spinner, updating as it goes, and a "Stop" button. Once it ends, the panel lists its proposals, as below, then "Accept all" and "Reject all" buttons along the panel's foot, and closes itself once none is left to decide. A run that fails shows its error, as the states say, and leaves the request in its box to try again; one that proposes nothing says so, as an empty state. Proposals are kept until each is decided, so closing Planning and coming back finds them as they were.
Drafting can also be started from an open story, with "Ask agent" in its card's menu or the open story's footer: the request box then reads "What does this story need?", the run is told the story's title and prompt, and the drafting panel takes the open story's place in the side panel, the story's card staying highlighted while the draft is about it.
A draft about a story, from "Ask agent" or "Draft with agent", may propose rewriting it: a proposal whose target is the story itself, to be rewritten, its title, prompt, mode, and spec citations replaced by the proposal's, its steps and attachments kept; as well as additions to it, each a step, as the proposals say.
The request is sent with the panel's "Draft" button, its primary action, or by pressing Enter in the request box, whichever label it reads; Ctrl+Enter there starts it too, and the button's tooltip gives Enter as its shortcut. Shift+Enter adds a new line in the box and sends nothing. A request that is empty, or only spaces and new lines, starts nothing, and the button is disabled while it is. While a run is going, Enter starts no second one, and the button is disabled. Enter in the request box only ever sends the request: the list's Enter, opening or closing the focused story, never fires while focus is in the box.

## Drafting With Agent

Several stories can be drafted at once, each in a run of its own. "Draft with agent" is an item of the menu of every card in Draft, the Backlog, and Next sprint, the menu its hover button opens and right-clicking opens at the pointer, as the parts say, and a button of the bulk bar, drafting every selected story; a card in Current sprint or Done, and a finding, has no such item. It is meant mainly for Draft's rough stories, but drafts any story it is offered on.
Each story drafts as "Ask agent" drafts an open story, with no request box and nothing to type: the run is told the story's title, its prompt, its mode, and its steps, and asked to turn it into a full story, a fuller prompt, the mode that fits it, the steps it needs, and its spec citations, grounded as the spec grounding says. Each run is its own, in a run of its own with no session, a Question run as the proposals say, with the planning prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts). All the runs start at once, none waiting on another, however many stories are drafted; one ending, failing, or being stopped never touches the others. A story already drafting is skipped, so drafting it again starts no second run.
The drafts never take over the side panel, which holds only one thing at a time, as the layout says: a story drafting this way shows its run on its own card, and opening the story while it drafts shows the story in the panel as ever, its run going on. While its run goes, the card's property row shows the loading state, as the states say, "Drafting…" with its spinner, in place of its status, and its menu has "Stop", which stops that run alone and keeps whatever it had proposed by then, in place of "Draft with agent". A run that fails shows its error on that card, beneath its property row, as the states say, in the theme's error colour with "Details" collapsed and "Try again", which drafts the story again; the error stays until the story is drafted again or opened. A run cut off when the application quits, as every run is, shows the same error once it is open again.
When a run ends, its card's property row shows how many of its proposals are waiting, as "3 proposals", in the theme's accent, or "1 proposal", counting down as each is decided and gone once none is left; a run that proposed nothing shows "Nothing proposed", muted, until the story is next opened. Opening the story shows them in the side panel, beneath the story's own fields, under a "Proposals" heading, as proposal cards, as the proposal cards say, with "Accept all" and "Reject all" beneath the last. Drafting a story again while proposals of its wait adds the new ones after them.
A story's waiting proposals are kept until each is decided, saved with the plan as soon as its run ends, in `.suspense/planning/proposals/`, a file per story named by its id, so they come back, and its card's count with them, after closing Planning, a reload, or a restart. A story deleted takes its waiting proposals with it, and one duplicated copies none.
Nothing reaches the plan until it is accepted, as the proposals say. Accepting the story's rewrite rewrites it as proposed, and accepting an addition adds its step to the story's end, as the proposal cards say. A story in Draft whose proposal is accepted, either kind, moves to the end of the Backlog, as "Move to backlog" moves it and motion animates it, staying open if it was; a story in the Backlog or Next sprint stays where it is. A story sent while its proposals waited can no longer be rewritten or added to, as the proposal cards say.

## Proposals

The agent never writes the plan itself. Its run is a Question run, as the [Sandbox](../sandbox/index.md#sandbox) says, able to read the project and change nothing, and Suspense data is never granted to it, so it can't touch `.suspense/planning/`; it only proposes, and nothing it proposes reaches the plan until the user accepts it.
Each proposal is one concrete task, with a title, a prompt, and the mode that fits it, and says where it should go: either "New story", to become a story of its own, or "Add to" a story, naming an existing story the run was shown, the open story first, to be added to it, or, in a draft about a story, "Rewrite", the story itself rewritten, as drafting says. The planning prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts) asks for each proposal as a fenced block whose info string is `suspense-story`, holding its title, its mode, its target, `new`, the story's id, or `rewrite`, its `spec` list of citations into the Piton source, as the spec grounding says, and its prompt, so the application reads it whatever else the reply says. The planning prompt explains the form with an example, as:
```suspense-story
title: Close a finished sprint
mode: chain
target: new
spec:
- spec/scope/planning/index.pi#Planning.sprint: when Close sprint shows
- No match: archiving a closed sprint
prompt:
Close sprint is offered once every story of the current sprint has
ended, as spec/scope/planning/index.pi#Planning.sprint says.
```
A block that names a story that no longer exists is shown as "New story", with a muted note saying so.

## Spec Grounding

A drafted story is grounded in the project's spec, never written from the request alone. Before proposing, the drafter searches the spec for what each story touches: the requirements and behaviours it changes or relies on, the names it uses, as a feature, a component, or a property, and the sections they are in. It reads the spec as every run does, from its compiled reference or from what `piton slice` prints, never from the Piton source, as the [ModeSystemPrompts](../prompt-mode/mode-system-prompts/index.md#mode-system-prompts) says, and the planning prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts) tells it to.
Each proposal cites what it found, specifically: its `suspense-story` block holds a `spec` list, one entry per element, each a link to the element in the spec's Piton source, under the spec location, never to the compiled reference: the .pi file, relative to the project directory, then `#` and the element it names, an anchor or a property on one, in the form `piton slice` takes, as `spec/scope/planning/index.pi#Planning.sprint`, with a short line of what in it the story touches. The story's prompt names those elements and links to them in the same form. Only elements the drafter found are cited, and where they point is the only thing that is Piton source: the drafter still learns what the spec says only from the compiled reference or from what `piton slice` prints, never by reading the Piton source, as the [ModeSystemPrompts](../prompt-mode/mode-system-prompts/index.md#mode-system-prompts) says, and works out each element's source from what `piton slice` prints for it, which names the file each declaration is in.
The application checks each link against the Piton source when the proposal arrives: the file has to exist under the spec location, and has to define the named element, the anchor declared in it and, for a property, that property on it, as `piton slice` would find it. A link that fails either check is dropped, with a muted note on the card saying which link it dropped and why, "no such file" or "the file doesn't define Planning.sprint". Each citation, on the proposal card and in an accepted story's "Spec" list, opens that place in the Piton source, the file in the editor at the element's declaration, as the [OpenFile](../open-file/index.md#open-file) opens a file.
Where no spec element matches part of a story, as a request for something the spec doesn't describe yet, the story says so instead of inventing one: its `spec` list holds an entry reading "No match", with what was looked for, and its prompt says plainly that the spec has nothing on it yet, so the change is new. A proposal with no element at all, and no "No match", isn't shown as a story: its card says the drafter cited no spec, in the theme's warning colour, with "Draft again", which runs the draft for that story once more.
The citations are shown on the proposal's card, beneath its prompt, as a short list, each element's name, as `Planning.sprint`, and its file, muted, as a link that opens that place in the Piton source, a "No match" entry muted with a small marker, and are kept with the story once it is accepted, listed in the open story beneath its steps as "Spec", and shown in its card's tooltip. They can be edited with the story, removed, or added to by hand: "Add citation" opens a picker of the spec's elements, its anchors and their properties, found from the Piton source and searched by name, never from the compiled reference, and adds the one picked in the same form, checked as a proposal's are.
Each citation says what kind of element it is, "Requirement", "Behaviour", "Name", or "Section", shown as a small muted label before it, and quotes the few words of the spec it rests on, so what the story relies on can be checked at a glance. Grounding holds for every proposal, however it came: from Draft stories, from research that ends with proposals, and from an Ask answer's `suspense-story` block, each checked and flagged the same way when it arrives.

## Proposal Cards

Each proposal is a card in the panel, in the order given: a label at its top, "New story" in the theme's accent, or, for an addition, "Add to this story" where it targets the story the research or draft was started from, "Rewrite this story" for a rewrite, and "Add to" and the story's title otherwise, in the story's own mode's colour, so where it will go is always read before anything else on the card; its title, its mode pill, and its prompt beneath, clipped at four lines with "Read more"; and three buttons along its foot, "Accept", "Edit", and "Reject", reachable by Tab and labelled for a screen reader. Edit opens the card's title, prompt, mode, and target in place, the target a menu of "New story" and each story, to be changed before it is accepted; editing doesn't accept it. Reject removes the card, with a short "Undo" in its place for a few seconds.
Accept records it in the plan straight away, saved as any change to the plan is: a new story goes into the Backlog, at its end, in its mode, marked "From agent" with a small sparkle beside its title until it is first opened, its spec citations kept with it, whether it was accepted on its own card or with "Accept all", and whether it came from drafting, research, or an Ask answer. Accepting never adds anything to Next sprint, so it never starts a sprint, however Start automatically is set; an accepted story reaches a sprint only once it is moved to Next sprint, as the sprint says; an addition is added to the named story as a new step at its end, as its steps say, its attachments with it, and the story is marked "Updated by agent" until it is next opened, the new step shown highlighted the first time it is; where the story no longer exists by then, the step isn't added, as the steps say, and the card stays, its target changed to "New story", to be accepted again; a rewrite replaces the story's title, prompt, mode, and spec citations with the proposal's, keeping its steps and attachments, and marks it "Updated by agent" the same way, and where the story no longer exists by then, it is changed to "New story" the same way. A story already sent can't be added to or rewritten: its proposals offer only "New story". Each card, once decided, goes from the panel, and an accepted one leaves a one-line muted note in its place, "Added to Backlog" or "Added to <story>", with a link that opens the story where it is, in the Backlog or its section.
Ask answers can propose the same way: a `suspense-story` block in an answer is shown as a proposal card, with the same Accept, Edit, and Reject, its accepting recording it in the plan as above, so a story can come out of a conversation without anything written silently.

## Research

The agent can be asked to research a topic before anything is planned or built: "Research", a button beside "Draft stories" on the Backlog's heading, Research on the [RibbonPlanningTab](../../ui/components/ribbon/planning-tab/index.md#ribbon-planning-tab), which opens Planning first, and "Research" in an open story's menu, open a box labelled "What should be looked into?". Research runs as a read-only investigation, a Question run, as the [Sandbox](../sandbox/index.md#sandbox) says, which can read the project and the web and change nothing, given the research prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts), which asks for findings, not changes: what was found, where, with links to the files and lines it read, what it is unsure of, and what it would suggest, and it may end with proposals, as the proposals say. Its box and its run are shown in the side panel, replacing whatever it held, as drafting's are, its live line of what it is reading, a Stop button, and its spinner, and its proposals in the same cards with the same buttons as drafting's, with Accept all and Reject all at its foot.
Its findings are a document of their own, "Research: <topic>", rendered as markdown with its links clickable, shown in the Findings column, the board's last, after Done, newest first, each a row with its topic, when it ran, and its state, and opening in a body tab of its own to read whole. Findings started from a story are also listed in that story, beneath its steps. They are saved with the plan, in `.suspense/planning/research/`, a markdown file each, so they can be read again later, and can be deleted from their row's menu.

## Paused

Once research ends, however it ended, planning is paused. While it is paused nothing is implemented: Send sprint, the ribbon's Send Sprint, and the sprint's Ctrl+Shift+Enter are disabled, their tooltips saying planning is paused for review, and no proposal from the research is added to the plan by itself. Stories can still be written, edited, ordered, and moved, and proposals still accepted or rejected one by one, since those only change the plan.
