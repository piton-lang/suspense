# Planning

## How To Build

A Planning should be built as a reusable component that will be reused throughout the rest of the codebase.  A component is configurable through arguments or properties that alter its behaviour, appearance, and details.  A Planning should encapsulate all of its own reusable behaviour, while leaving the wiring, integration, and integration to the domain of the application.

## Theme

Drawn in the [Theme](../../ui/theme/index.md#theme): its surfaces, text, lines, accent, and status colours are the theme's roles, named as the theme names them, in both dark and light mode, never colours of its own.

## Pitch

A plain, quick list of the work still to do, as Linear keeps issues: stories written ahead of time, each saying how it will be sent, Code, Chain, Spec, Ask, or Freeform, gathered into the next sprint and sent to the agent in order, all at once.

## Description

Planning opens in a tab of its own in the body, "Planning", with a checklist icon, as a file opens, as the [Body](../application/Body.md#body) says, from the ribbon's Planning tab or the palette's "Planning". It is a kanban board, filling the body beneath the tab bar, as the [Body](../application/Body.md#body) places it, from the tab bar down to the chat input and from the body's left edge to its right. Its sections sit side by side as columns, in the order work moves, left to right: "Backlog", "Next sprint", "Current sprint", and "Done", then "Findings", the last, as research says. Each column is headed by its name and count, with its actions, as the layout says, and holds its stories as cards, one beneath another. Every column is always there, whatever it holds; one with nothing in it shows its empty state, as the states say.

## Stories

A story is one piece of work, written as it will be sent: a title, a prompt, and how it will be sent. Each is a story card in its column, as the parts say, showing all a story shows: a drag grip at its top left; a small mode picker, a pill reading its mode, "Code", "Chain", "Spec", "Ask", or "Freeform", in that mode's colour, as the [PromptModes](../prompt-mode/modes/index.md#prompt-modes) colours it; once it has been sent, its status, as a task's is shown, at its top right; its title beneath, in the body's text colour, wrapping onto a second line and cut off with an ellipsis only after that; and beneath the title, muted, its step count and a small icon for anything attached, when it has them. Hovering a card shows a menu button at its top right. A story has no Send of its own, as the sprint says.
A column is too narrow to open a story in place, so clicking a card never expands it beneath itself or moves the cards below it: it opens the story in the side panel, docked at the board's right, as the layout places it and the parts draw a panel, headed by the story's title. The panel holds all an open story holds: its title, editable in place; its prompt, in a [GrowingTextInput](../../ui/components/growing-text-input/index.md#growing-text-input) with the chat input's highlighting, completions, and slicing, as the [ChatInput](../prompt-mode/chat-input/index.md#chat-input) has, and its attachments beneath; its parameters, its mode, model, effort, and Slice, as the chat input shows them; its steps, with Add step, as its steps say; and its Spec citations, as the spec grounding says. Every change is saved as it is made. While its story is open, its card stays highlighted, its line in the theme's accent, so which story the panel shows is always plain. Only one story is open at a time: clicking another card opens that one in the panel instead. Escape, the panel's close button, or clicking the open story's card again closes it, and its card's highlight with it.

## Creating

Creating a story is quick. A "New story" line at the foot of Next sprint, and Ctrl+N (Cmd+N on macOS) while Planning has focus, adds a story at the end of Next sprint, open, its title focused, in the mode last picked for a story. However a story is added, it goes into Next sprint, at its end: a new story, a duplicate, and one added from an Ask answer's card with "Add to sprint" alike. It can then be moved to the Backlog to wait, as the sprint says. The one exception is an accepted proposal for a new story, from drafting, research, or an Ask answer's proposal card, which goes into the Backlog, at its end, as the proposal cards say, and reaches a sprint only once it is moved to Next sprint. Typing a title and pressing Enter adds it and starts another beneath it, so a list can be written down without the mouse; Escape on an empty one removes it. A title with no prompt sends its title as its prompt.
A story can also be prompted into existence: "Draft stories", a button beside the Backlog's heading, opens a small box to say what is wanted, in a sentence or a paragraph, and asks the harness, in a run of its own with no session, with the planning prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts), to break it into stories, each with a title, a prompt, and the mode that fits it. They come back as proposals, as the drafting and proposals say, each to be accepted, edited, or rejected, and none reaches the plan until it is accepted. An Ask answer's prompt cards also offer "Add to sprint", which adds the card's prompt as a story in its mode to Next sprint.

## Sprint

Stories move between the Backlog and Next sprint by dragging, as the [DragReorderBehaviour](../../ui/behaviours/drag-reorder/index.md#drag-reorder-behaviour) says, a card dragged by its grip from one column into a gap in the other as within either, the insertion line drawn across the column the pointer is over; an empty one takes a drop at its top. Dragged near the board's left or right edge, the board scrolls sideways, and near a column's top or bottom, that column scrolls, as dragging says. Only the Backlog and Next sprint take drops: Current sprint and Done show no insertion line, a story dropped on them stays where it was, and their cards can't be picked up, nor can a finding. Stories move too from a card's menu, "Move to next sprint" or "Move to backlog". Their order within Next sprint is the order they will be sent. A card's menu also has "Duplicate" and "Delete".
"Send sprint", a primary button at the right of Next sprint's heading, enabled while it holds a story, sends every story in it, in order, as though each had been written in the chat input on its mode's tab and sent, one after another, as the [PromptSending](../prompt-mode/sending/index.md#prompt-sending) says: each task queues in its lane behind the one before it, in the order of the sprint, and each question runs at once. The stories go into the queue as one block, so nothing sent later comes between them, and the queue's auto-send carries them on as ever, as the [QueueList](../prompt-mode/queue-list/index.md#queue-list) says.
Each story starts a conversation of its own, as though New conversation had been pressed just before it, and so does each later step of a story's chain: a story never carries on the conversation of the story before it, nor of anything its lane ran before the sprint. New conversation, pressed before the sprint was sent, is left for the next prompt written in the chat input.
A story can't be sent on its own. The only way a story reaches work is through the sprint: a story in the Backlog waits until it is moved into Next sprint and the sprint is sent, and a story in Next sprint goes when the sprint does. Nothing in Planning, its cards, their menus, the open story, the keyboard, the palette, or the ribbon, sends a single story, and nothing elsewhere sends a story by any other route: a story can't be dragged or copied into the chat input or the queue to be sent from there, and its menu has no "Send" or "Edit in chat input". The chat input's own prompts are not stories and are sent as ever; once a prompt is a story, the sprint is its only way to work. To send one story at once, it is the only story in Next sprint when Send sprint is pressed. A story that failed or was cancelled is sent again the same way, with the next sprint.
Sending the sprint moves its stories into the "Current sprint" column, to the right of Next sprint, so Next sprint is empty again and stories added meanwhile go there, to be the sprint after. Current sprint and Done are columns of the board whatever they hold, never appearing, disappearing, or collapsing; while no sprint is under way Current sprint is empty and shows its empty state, as Done does before anything is done. A story in Current sprint keeps its place, its card showing its task's status as it queues, runs, and ends, and clicking it opens its task in the Tasks tab; it can't be moved or edited there. Once every story of Current sprint has ended, its column's heading offers "Close sprint", which moves the stories that are done into the Done column, their cards muted, and those that failed, were cancelled, or stopped to the start of Next sprint, in their order, to be sent again. While a sprint is current, Send sprint is disabled, its tooltip saying a sprint is already under way.
Send sprint only ever moves into Current sprint the stories it actually sent. A story with nothing to send, its title and its prompt both empty, is skipped: it stays in Next sprint, where it was, and Send sprint's result says, muted, how many it skipped and why, as "1 empty story left in Next sprint". A sprint where every story is empty sends nothing and makes no Current sprint.
Once a sprint is done, it can always be closed. Every story of Current sprint counts as ended once its task has ended, done, failed, cancelled, or stopped, or once its task can no longer be found, as when its record was deleted, it was never recorded, or the application quit before it was. A story whose task is missing is never shown as Queued, or as anything still to come: its card reads "Task missing", muted, in the theme's warning colour, its tooltip saying its task can't be found, and it counts as ended and not Done. A story still waiting in its lane's queue, or held for review, as the paused section says, is still to come and hasn't ended. So Close sprint shows as soon as no story of Current sprint is building, compiling, running, paused, queued, or held, and never waits on a task that can't arrive.
Close sprint treats a story whose task is missing as it treats one that failed or was cancelled: it goes back to the start of Next sprint, with the others sent again, in their order, never into Done. Closing saves the sections, Done, Next sprint, the Backlog, and the emptied Current sprint, to `order.json` straight away, as the [ProjectData](../project-data/index.md#project-data) says, and Current sprint's state is worked out afresh from the stories' tasks whenever the plan is read, so a sprint that finished, or whose tasks went missing, before a reload or a restart can still be closed after it, as before.

## Auto Start

Next sprint can start on its own: a "Start automatically" switch on Next sprint's heading, beside Send sprint, its tooltip saying the next sprint starts as soon as the current one is done. It starts off, and is saved with the plan, in `order.json`, so it is kept across reloads and restarts.
While it is on, once every story of Current sprint has ended Done, the current sprint closes by itself, as Close sprint closes it, and Next sprint, if it holds a story, is sent at once, as Send sprint sends it, with nobody having to start it; and while no sprint is current, a story added to an empty Next sprint starts it the same way. A sprint that ended with a story failed, cancelled, stopped, or with its task missing, never starts the next by itself: it waits, Close sprint offered as ever, and the switch's row says, in the theme's warning colour, why the next didn't start. Nor does the next start while planning is paused, as the paused section says: it starts once the pause is ended by Resume, or Approve, if the switch is still on. An empty Next sprint starts nothing.
Each time a sprint starts by itself, a passive notification says so, "Next sprint started: 4 stories", with a link that opens Planning, and the debug log records it. Turning the switch off never stops a sprint already sent; it only keeps the next from starting by itself. Turning it on while no sprint is current and Next sprint holds a story starts Next sprint at once, as adding a story to it would, unless planning is paused.
The switch works while Planning isn't open, from the plan as saved, whichever tab is shown. When the project is opened, or the application starts, with the switch on, no sprint current, and Next sprint holding a story, it doesn't start by itself; the switch's row says, muted, "Waiting to start", with a "Start now" button, so a sprint never begins unseen as the application opens. A current sprint whose stories were cut off when the application quit, as every run is, ended them, and never starts the next by itself.
While the switch is on, Next sprint's heading shows "Auto" beside its count, in the theme's accent, and the ribbon's Send Sprint button's tooltip says the next sprint starts automatically.

## Saving

The stories, their order, and which section each is in are saved with the project's data, as the [ProjectData](../project-data/index.md#project-data) says, in `.suspense/planning/`, a file per story, holding its hidden anchor as a queued prompt's is, and an `order.json` listing the sections and their stories, so the plan is kept with the project and comes back as it was left. A story's steps are saved in its own file, in their order, as its steps say, never apart from it.

## Steps

A story can have steps: one or more further prompts, each a piece of the story done after the one before, as a chain's steps are. Each step has a prompt, a mode of its own, Code or Spec, starting as the story's, and a model and effort, starting as the story's, and may have attachments. A story with no steps is just its own prompt, as before; its prompt is always its first step.
An open story lists its steps beneath its prompt, in order, each a row numbered from 2, the story's own prompt being 1, with its mode pill and the first line of its prompt, opening in place to edit, as a story does. "Add step", beneath the last, adds one at the end, open and focused; several can be added one after another, Enter in an empty step's prompt adding none. Steps are reordered by dragging, as the [DragReorderBehaviour](../../ui/behaviours/drag-reorder/index.md#drag-reorder-behaviour) says, or Alt+Up and Alt+Down, and each has a remove button in its row and in its menu. The story's own prompt stays first and can't be moved among them.
A step belongs to its story and to nothing else. It is saved in the story's file, in a `steps` list of the story's hidden anchor, in its order, and saved as every change to the plan is, straight away; a story duplicated copies its steps, and one deleted deletes them. Its order is kept wherever the story goes, between sections, into a sprint, and back from Done.
Wherever the story is shown, its steps are too: its card in Planning shows how many beneath its title, muted, as "3 steps", with the list in its tooltip; the open story lists them as above; a proposal to add to it shows them, so what it would add to can be seen; and once it is sent, it is sent as a chain, its prompt first and each step after it in order, each step sent only once the one before it finishes, as the [PromptEditor](../prompt-mode/index.md#prompt-editor)'s chained tasks are, so the Tasks tab shows the story as one chain with its steps beneath it. A story sent can't be given more steps; its steps, sent or still to come, read as they were sent. In Current sprint, a story with steps lists its steps on its card, beneath its title, a line for each step, in order, each with its number, the first line of its prompt cut off with an ellipsis, and its own status, as a running chain's are in the Tasks tab; in Done, and in a finding started from the story, its steps are listed the same way, collapsed to its step count until it is expanded. The same steps, in the same order, are what every place shows, since every place reads them from the story's own file.
A step is only ever added to a story that exists. Adding one to a story that doesn't, as one deleted while a proposal naming it waited, or by another window, or whose file was removed from `.suspense/planning/` meanwhile, changes nothing: no story is made for it, no other story is given it, and nothing is saved. It says so in plain words where it was asked for, as "That story no longer exists, so the step wasn't added", in the theme's error colour, as the states say, with what was to be added kept, so it can be added to another story or as a new one. A step added to a story already sent is refused the same way, saying the story has been sent.

## Layout

Everything in Planning is laid out on one rhythm, so the board scans at a glance. The board fills the body beneath the tab bar, with 24 pixels of padding at its top, sides, and foot. Its columns are all the same fixed width, 320 pixels, 16 pixels apart, each as tall as the board, set side by side from its left edge in the order work moves, Backlog, Next sprint, Current sprint, Done, and Findings, the last. A column has no background or border of its own; its cards and the gaps between columns set it apart.
Each column's heading is a row 32 pixels tall at its top: its name in small semibold text, its count beside it, muted, and its actions at its right, each a labelled button, never an icon alone, as the sections give them: "Draft stories" and "Research" on the Backlog; "Send sprint" and "Start automatically" on Next sprint; "Close sprint" on Current sprint, once its sprint can close. A heading with more actions than fit its width wraps them onto a second line beneath its name rather than cutting any off. 8 pixels beneath the heading come its cards, one beneath another, 8 pixels apart, each the column's full width.
Each column scrolls up and down on its own, its heading staying at its top while its cards scroll beneath it, with the [Scrollbar](../../ui/components/scrollbar/index.md#scrollbar) shown along its right while it holds more than fits. The board scrolls sideways when its columns are wider than the tab, with the [Scrollbar](../../ui/components/scrollbar/index.md#scrollbar) along its foot; while they fit, it doesn't scroll. Every card's state reads the same way, as below, and every label is the same small text, muted where it is secondary.
The side panel is the one place anything opens: an open story, Draft stories, and Research, never beneath a heading or a card, and never in a popup or over the board. It is docked at the right of Planning's tab, 400 pixels wide, as tall as the tab, from the tab bar down to the chat input, a 1 pixel line in the theme's line colour along its left edge, and it scrolls up and down on its own, its heading staying at its top, with the [Scrollbar](../../ui/components/scrollbar/index.md#scrollbar) along its right while it holds more than fits. Only one thing is in it at a time: opening a story, drafting, or research replaces whatever it held, and closing it leaves nothing there. While it is open the board narrows to the space left of it, its columns keeping their width and scrolling sideways as ever when they no longer fit; the panel never covers the board, and the board never covers it. Closed, it takes no space and the board fills the tab again.
While paused, the pause bar runs across the top of the whole board, above every column, as the paused section says, beside the side panel when it is open, never over it. Each column is headed as above, so nothing sits outside the rhythm, and an open story, a drafting or research panel, and the citations and steps inside a story all use the same four parts, below.

## Parts

Everything in Planning is built from four parts, each always drawn the same way:
- A story card: a box the full width of its column, on the theme's raised surface, with a 1 pixel line in the theme's line colour, corners rounded 6 pixels, 12 pixels of padding, 8 pixels from the next; a top line with its drag grip, 16 pixels, and its mode pill at its left and its status and time, right-aligned, at its right, its menu button beside them on hover and always while it is focused; its title beneath, in up to two lines, cut off with an ellipsis after the second; then, muted, its step count and attachment icon, when it has them; and, in Current sprint, its steps' lines beneath, as its steps say; used for every story, in every column, muted in Done
- A row: 36 pixels tall, for steps in an open story and for findings; its secondary text muted and cut off with an ellipsis, its buttons small ghost buttons with labels, shown at its right on hover and always while it is focused
- A panel: the side panel, docked at the board's right, full height, as the layout places it, on the theme's raised surface, its corners square, 12 pixels of padding, a heading row of 32 pixels with its name, as "Draft stories", "Research", or the story's title, and its close button, its content beneath, and its actions in a footer row, right-aligned, the primary action last; used for an open story, drafting, and research
- A card: a box inside a panel, on the theme's base surface, with a 1 pixel line in the theme's line colour, corners rounded 4 pixels, 12 pixels of padding, 8 pixels from the next; a label row at its top, its content, and a footer row of labelled buttons; used for proposals and for each finding's summary
Labels are words, never icons alone: every button reads what it does, "Accept", "Send sprint", "Start automatically", and an icon beside a label only repeats it. Each heading's count is a plain number, muted, and each status a coloured label, as the states say. The pause bar is a row across the whole board's top, above its columns, 40 pixels tall, its text and buttons on one line, the same height whatever it says.

## States

Each part of Planning has the same few states, shown the same way wherever they come up, so a state is never guessed from what is missing.
- Empty: a single muted line, centred in the column beneath its heading, saying what is missing and what to do, as "Nothing planned. Drag stories here, or press Ctrl+N.", with no illustration
- Loading: a spinner and a short muted label of what is happening, as "Drafting stories…", in the place the result will appear, never a blank space
- Paused: a story whose task is paused shows "Paused" as its status, in the theme's warning colour, with a small pause icon, and a "Resume" button on its card, as the PromptSending says of pausing; a sprint with any story paused says so in its heading, as "1 paused", in the same colour
- Error: a line in the theme's error colour, saying what went wrong in plain words, with what the harness or the application reported beneath it collapsed under "Details", and a "Try again" button where trying again can help
A story's status on its card is the same coloured label a task has, as the [MessageList](../prompt-mode/message-list/index.md#message-list)'s status says, Queued, Running with its spinner and time, Done, Failed, Cancelled, Paused, or, where its task can't be found, Task missing, as the sprint says, and a story not yet sent shows nothing there, so its absence means "not sent".
Each part shows each state that can come up for it, worded for it:
- Story list: empty, "Nothing planned. Press Ctrl+N to add a story." in Next sprint, "Nothing waiting." in the Backlog, "No sprint under way. Send sprint to start one." in Current sprint, and "Nothing done yet." in Done; loading, while the plan is read on opening, a few skeleton cards in each column, never an empty column; error, when the plan can't be read or saved, saying which file and why, with "Try again"
- Research and its findings: loading, the live line of what it is reading with its spinner; empty, "No findings yet." in Findings; error, the run's failure with "Try again", which keeps the question in its box; a finding that ended early, as stopped, marked "Incomplete", muted, in its row
- Pause: the pause bar, in the theme's warning colour, as the paused section says, and the sprint's Send buttons disabled with their tooltips saying why; a sprint that can't start for a failed story says so in its heading, in the same colour
- Proposals: loading, the drafting line and spinner; empty, "No stories proposed." with "Draft again"; error, the run's failure with "Try again"; a proposal with no spec cited, in the warning colour with "Draft again", as the spec grounding says

## Drafting

Drafting stories shows its work in one place, the side panel, docked at the board's right, as the layout places it, replacing whatever it held, never in a popup that hides the list. It has the request box at its top, labelled "Describe what you want", and beneath it, while the run goes, its research: a muted line of what the harness is doing, as "Reading the spec…", with its spinner, updating as it goes, and a "Stop" button. Once it ends, the panel lists its proposals, as below, then "Accept all" and "Reject all" buttons along the panel's foot, and closes itself once none is left to decide. A run that fails shows its error, as the states say, and leaves the request in its box to try again; one that proposes nothing says so, as an empty state. Proposals are kept until each is decided, so closing Planning and coming back finds them as they were.
Drafting can also be started from an open story, with "Ask agent" in its card's menu or the open story's footer: the request box then reads "What does this story need?", the run is told the story's title and prompt, and the drafting panel takes the open story's place in the side panel, the story's card staying highlighted while the draft is about it.
The request is sent with the panel's "Draft" button, its primary action, or by pressing Enter in the request box, whichever label it reads; Ctrl+Enter there starts it too, and the button's tooltip gives Enter as its shortcut. Shift+Enter adds a new line in the box and sends nothing. A request that is empty, or only spaces and new lines, starts nothing, and the button is disabled while it is. While a run is going, Enter starts no second one, and the button is disabled. Enter in the request box only ever sends the request: the list's Enter, opening or closing the focused story, never fires while focus is in the box.

## Proposals

The agent never writes the plan itself. Its run is a Question run, as the [Sandbox](../sandbox/index.md#sandbox) says, able to read the project and change nothing, and Suspense data is never granted to it, so it can't touch `.suspense/planning/`; it only proposes, and nothing it proposes reaches the plan until the user accepts it.
Each proposal is one concrete task, with a title, a prompt, and the mode that fits it, and says where it should go: either "New story", to become a story of its own, or "Add to" a story, naming an existing story the run was shown, the open story first, to be added to it. The planning prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts) asks for each proposal as a fenced block whose info string is `suspense-story`, holding its title, its mode, its target, `new` or the story's id, its `spec` list of citations into the Piton source, as the spec grounding says, and its prompt, so the application reads it whatever else the reply says. The planning prompt explains the form with an example, as:
```suspense-story
title: Close a finished sprint
mode: chain
target: new
spec:
- spec/scope/planning/index.pi#Planning.sprint: when Close sprint shows
- No match: archiving a closed sprint
prompt:
Close sprint is offered once every story of the current sprint has
ended, as spec/scope/planning/index.pi#Planning.sprint says.
```
A block that names a story that no longer exists is shown as "New story", with a muted note saying so.

## Spec Grounding

A drafted story is grounded in the project's spec, never written from the request alone. Before proposing, the drafter searches the spec for what each story touches: the requirements and behaviours it changes or relies on, the names it uses, as a feature, a component, or a property, and the sections they are in. It reads the spec as every run does, from its compiled reference or from what `piton slice` prints, never from the Piton source, as the [ModeSystemPrompts](../prompt-mode/mode-system-prompts/index.md#mode-system-prompts) says, and the planning prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts) tells it to.
Each proposal cites what it found, specifically: its `suspense-story` block holds a `spec` list, one entry per element, each a link to the element in the spec's Piton source, under the spec location, never to the compiled reference: the .pi file, relative to the project directory, then `#` and the element it names, an anchor or a property on one, in the form `piton slice` takes, as `spec/scope/planning/index.pi#Planning.sprint`, with a short line of what in it the story touches. The story's prompt names those elements and links to them in the same form. Only elements the drafter found are cited, and where they point is the only thing that is Piton source: the drafter still learns what the spec says only from the compiled reference or from what `piton slice` prints, never by reading the Piton source, as the [ModeSystemPrompts](../prompt-mode/mode-system-prompts/index.md#mode-system-prompts) says, and works out each element's source from what `piton slice` prints for it, which names the file each declaration is in.
The application checks each link against the Piton source when the proposal arrives: the file has to exist under the spec location, and has to define the named element, the anchor declared in it and, for a property, that property on it, as `piton slice` would find it. A link that fails either check is dropped, with a muted note on the card saying which link it dropped and why, "no such file" or "the file doesn't define Planning.sprint". Each citation, on the proposal card and in an accepted story's "Spec" list, opens that place in the Piton source, the file in the editor at the element's declaration, as the [OpenFile](../open-file/index.md#open-file) opens a file.
Where no spec element matches part of a story, as a request for something the spec doesn't describe yet, the story says so instead of inventing one: its `spec` list holds an entry reading "No match", with what was looked for, and its prompt says plainly that the spec has nothing on it yet, so the change is new. A proposal with no element at all, and no "No match", isn't shown as a story: its card says the drafter cited no spec, in the theme's warning colour, with "Draft again", which runs the draft for that story once more.
The citations are shown on the proposal's card, beneath its prompt, as a short list, each element's name, as `Planning.sprint`, and its file, muted, as a link that opens that place in the Piton source, a "No match" entry muted with a small marker, and are kept with the story once it is accepted, listed in the open story beneath its steps as "Spec", and shown in its card's tooltip. They can be edited with the story, removed, or added to by hand: "Add citation" opens a picker of the spec's elements, its anchors and their properties, found from the Piton source and searched by name, never from the compiled reference, and adds the one picked in the same form, checked as a proposal's are.
Each citation says what kind of element it is, "Requirement", "Behaviour", "Name", or "Section", shown as a small muted label before it, and quotes the few words of the spec it rests on, so what the story relies on can be checked at a glance. Grounding holds for every proposal, however it came: from Draft stories, from research that ends with proposals, and from an Ask answer's `suspense-story` block, each checked and flagged the same way when it arrives.

## Proposal Cards

Each proposal is a card in the panel, in the order given: a label at its top, "New story" in the theme's accent, or, for an addition, "Add to this story" where it targets the story the research or draft was started from, and "Add to" and the story's title otherwise, in the story's own mode's colour, so where it will go is always read before anything else on the card; its title, its mode pill, and its prompt beneath, clipped at four lines with "Read more"; and three buttons along its foot, "Accept", "Edit", and "Reject", reachable by Tab and labelled for a screen reader. Edit opens the card's title, prompt, mode, and target in place, the target a menu of "New story" and each story, to be changed before it is accepted; editing doesn't accept it. Reject removes the card, with a short "Undo" in its place for a few seconds.
Accept records it in the plan straight away, saved as any change to the plan is: a new story goes into the Backlog, at its end, in its mode, marked "From agent" with a small sparkle beside its title until it is first opened, its spec citations kept with it, whether it was accepted on its own card or with "Accept all", and whether it came from drafting, research, or an Ask answer. Accepting never adds anything to Next sprint, so it never starts a sprint, however Start automatically is set; an accepted story reaches a sprint only once it is moved to Next sprint, as the sprint says; an addition is added to the named story as a new step at its end, as its steps say, its attachments with it, and the story is marked "Updated by agent" until it is next opened, the new step shown highlighted the first time it is; where the story no longer exists by then, the step isn't added, as the steps say, and the card stays, its target changed to "New story", to be accepted again. A story already sent can't be added to: its proposals offer only "New story". Each card, once decided, goes from the panel, and an accepted one leaves a one-line muted note in its place, "Added to Backlog" or "Added to <story>", with a link that opens the story where it is, in the Backlog or its section.
Ask answers can propose the same way: a `suspense-story` block in an answer is shown as a proposal card, with the same Accept, Edit, and Reject, its accepting recording it in the plan as above, so a story can come out of a conversation without anything written silently.

## Research

The agent can be asked to research a topic before anything is planned or built: "Research", a button beside "Draft stories" on the Backlog's heading, and "Research" in an open story's menu, open a box labelled "What should be looked into?". Research runs as a read-only investigation, a Question run, as the [Sandbox](../sandbox/index.md#sandbox) says, which can read the project and the web and change nothing, given the research prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts), which asks for findings, not changes: what was found, where, with links to the files and lines it read, what it is unsure of, and what it would suggest, and it may end with proposals, as the proposals say. Its box and its run are shown in the side panel, replacing whatever it held, as drafting's are, its live line of what it is reading, a Stop button, and its spinner, and its proposals in the same cards with the same buttons as drafting's, with Accept all and Reject all at its foot.
Its findings are a document of their own, "Research: <topic>", rendered as markdown with its links clickable, shown in the Findings column, the board's last, after Done, newest first, each a row with its topic, when it ran, and its state, and opening in a body tab of its own to read whole. Findings started from a story are also listed in that story, beneath its steps. They are saved with the plan, in `.suspense/planning/research/`, a markdown file each, so they can be read again later, and can be deleted from their row's menu.

## Paused

Once research ends, however it ended, planning is paused. While it is paused nothing is implemented: Send sprint, the ribbon's Send Sprint, and the sprint's Ctrl+Shift+Enter are disabled, their tooltips saying planning is paused for review, and no proposal from the research is added to the plan by itself. Stories can still be written, edited, ordered, and moved, and proposals still accepted or rejected one by one, since those only change the plan.
Nothing of the plan starts while it is paused, not only nothing new: a story of Current sprint still waiting in the queue, not yet started, is held where it is, as a queued prompt being edited is held, as the [QueueList](../prompt-mode/queue-list/index.md#queue-list) says, its card reading "Held for review", muted, and its lane waits on it; a story already running when the pause began runs on to its end, since stopping it would leave its work half done, and the bar says how many are still running. A chain's next step, as a story's steps are sent, is held the same way. Once the pause ends, by Resume or Approve, what was held goes on in its order, as though nothing had stopped it.
The pause is plain to see: a bar across the top of the whole board, above every column, in the theme's warning colour, with a pause icon, reads "Paused for review", names the research it is waiting on, with a link that opens its findings, and holds two buttons, "Resume", which ends the pause, and "Approve and send sprint", which ends it and sends Next sprint at once. The Planning tab in the body shows a small pause icon beside its name, and the ribbon's Planning tab a warning dot, while it is paused. Pressing either button is the only way the pause ends; nothing the agent does, and no time passing, ends it. A second research while paused keeps the pause, naming the latest.
The pause is saved with the plan, in `order.json`, with the research it waits on, as soon as it begins, so it is still paused, and still shown so, after the project is closed and opened again, the application restarted, or the window reloaded, until it is resumed or approved.

## Keyboard

Everything in Planning can be done from the keyboard. Tab moves through the columns' heading buttons, the cards and rows, and the open story's fields in order, and the focused one shows the theme's focus ring. In a column, Up and Down move between its cards and rows, and Left and Right move focus to the column beside it, onto its card or row nearest the focused one's height, skipping a column with none, and scrolling the board sideways to show it; Enter opens or closes the focused story, A accepts the focused proposal, E edits it, and R rejects it. Alt+Up and Alt+Down move the focused story up or down within its column, and Alt+Left and Alt+Right move it into the column beside it, as dragging does, where that column takes it: from the Backlog Alt+Right moves it to the end of Next sprint, and from Next sprint Alt+Left moves it to the end of the Backlog, focus going with it; anywhere else, as a story in Current sprint or Done, or toward a column that takes no drops, they do nothing. Ctrl+N adds a story, Ctrl+Enter moves the focused story to Next sprint, or to the Backlog from it, Ctrl+Shift+Enter sends the sprint, and M opens the focused story's mode picker, whose options are picked with the arrow keys and Enter. Delete deletes the focused story after a confirmation. Escape closes what is open, a menu, then the side panel, whether it holds a story, drafting, or research, one at a time. Every button and control has a label a screen reader reads, as its visible text or, for an icon, its tooltip, and each shortcut is given in the tooltip of the control it stands for.
Ctrl+Shift+D opens Draft stories, focusing its request box, where Enter starts the draft, as Ctrl+Enter also does, and Shift+Enter adds a new line; Ctrl+Shift+R opens Research, focusing its box, where Ctrl+Enter starts it; F6 moves focus between the board's regions, the pause bar, each column, the side panel when it is open, and back, so each is a single stop; within the pause bar, Tab reaches Resume and Approve and send sprint; within an open story, Tab reaches its steps, its Add step, and its Spec citations, each citation opening on Enter; and within Findings, Enter opens the focused finding in its tab. The Start automatically switch is reached by Tab on Next sprint's heading and toggled with Space. Focus never lands on something hidden or disabled, and after an action it stays where it was or moves to what the action made, as the new story or the next proposal, never back to the top.
