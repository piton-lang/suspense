42-A story can't be sent on its own. 44-Send sprint only ever moves into Current sprint the stories it actually sent. 45-Once a sprint is done, it can always be closed. A story still waiting in its lane's queue, or held for review, as the paused section says, is still to come and hasn't ended. 77-## Steps
78-
79-A story can have steps: one or more further prompts, each a piece of the story done after the one before, as a chain's steps are. Each step has a prompt, a mode of its own, Code or Spec, starting as the story's, and a model and effort, starting as the story's, and may have attachments. A story with no steps is just its own prompt, as before; its prompt is always its first step.
80-An open story lists its steps beneath its prompt, in order, each a row numbered from 2, the story's own prompt being 1, with its mode pill and the first line of its prompt, opening in place to edit, as a story does. "Add step", beneath the last, adds one at the end, open and focused; several can be added one after another, Enter in an empty step's prompt adding none. Steps are reordered by dragging, as the [DragReorderBehaviour](../../ui/behaviours/drag-reorder/index.md#drag-reorder-behaviour) says, or Alt+Up and Alt+Down, and each has a remove button in its row and in its menu. The story's own prompt stays first and can't be moved among them.
81-A step belongs to its story and to nothing else. It is saved in the story's file, in a `steps` list of the story's hidden anchor, in its order, and saved as every change to the plan is, straight away; a story duplicated copies its steps, and one deleted deletes them. Its order is kept wherever the story goes, between sections, into a sprint, and back from Done.
82-Wherever the story is shown, its steps are too: its card in Planning shows how many beneath its title, muted, as "3 steps", with the list in its tooltip; the open story lists them as above; a proposal to add to it shows them, so what it would add to can be seen; and once it is sent, it is sent as a chain, its prompt first and each step after it in order, each step sent only once the one before it finishes, as the [PromptEditor](../prompt-mode/index.md#prompt-editor)'s chained tasks are, so the Tasks tab shows the story as one chain with its steps beneath it. A story sent can't be given more steps; its steps, sent or still to come, read as they were sent. In Current sprint, a story with steps lists its steps on its card, beneath its title, a line for each step, in order, each with its number, the first line of its prompt cut off with an ellipsis, and its own status, as a running chain's are in the Tasks tab; in Done, and in a finding started from the story, its steps are listed the same way, collapsed to its step count until it is expanded. The same steps, in the same order, are what every place shows, since every place reads them from the story's own file.
83-A step is only ever added to a story that exists. Adding one to a story that doesn't, as one deleted while a proposal naming it waited, or by another window, or whose file was removed from `.suspense/planning/` meanwhile, changes nothing: no story is made for it, no other story is given it, and nothing is saved. It says so in plain words where it was asked for, as "That story no longer exists, so the step wasn't added", in the theme's error colour, as the states say, with what was to be added kept, so it can be added to another story or as a new one. A step added to a story already sent is refused the same way, saying the story has been sent.

Shift+Enter adds a new line in the box and sends nothing. A request that is empty, or only spaces and new lines, starts nothing, and the button is disabled while it is. While a run is going, Enter starts no second one, and the button is disabled. Enter in the request box only ever sends the request: the list's Enter, opening or closing the focused story, never fires while focus is in the box.

128-## Proposals
129-
130-The agent never writes the plan itself. Its run is a Question run, as the [Sandbox](../sandbox/index.md#sandbox) says, able to read the project and change nothing, and Suspense data is never granted to it, so it can't touch `.suspense/planning/`; it only proposes, and nothing it proposes reaches the plan until the user accepts it.
131-Each proposal is one concrete task, with a title, a prompt, and the mode that fits it, and says where it should go: either "New story", to become a story of its own, or "Add to" a story, naming an existing story the run was shown, the open story first, to be added to it. The planning prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts) asks for each proposal as a fenced block whose info string is `suspense-story`, holding its title, its mode, its target, `new` or the story's id, its `spec` list of citations into the Piton source, as the spec grounding says, and its prompt, so the application reads it whatever else the reply says. The planning prompt explains the form with an example, as:
132-```suspense-story
133-title: Close a finished sprint
134-mode: chain
135-target: new
136-spec:
137-- spec/scope/planning/index.pi#Planning.sprint: when Close sprint shows
138-- No match: archiving a closed sprint
139-prompt:
140-Close sprint is offered once every story of the current sprint has
141-ended, as spec/scope/planning/index.pi#Planning.sprint says.
142-```
143-A block that names a story that no longer exists is shown as "New story", with a muted note saying so.

145-## Spec Grounding
146-
147-A drafted story is grounded in the project's spec, never written from the request alone. Before proposing, the drafter searches the spec for what each story touches: the requirements and behaviours it changes or relies on, the names it uses, as a feature, a component, or a property, and the sections they are in. It reads the spec as every run does, from its compiled reference or from what `piton slice` prints, never from the Piton source, as the [ModeSystemPrompts](../prompt-mode/mode-system-prompts/index.md#mode-system-prompts) says, and the planning prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts) tells it to.
148-Each proposal cites what it found, specifically: its `suspense-story` block holds a `spec` list, one entry per element, each a link to the element in the spec's Piton source, under the spec location, never to the compiled reference: the .pi file, relative to the project directory, then `#` and the element it names, an anchor or a property on one, in the form `piton slice` takes, as `spec/scope/planning/index.pi#Planning.sprint`, with a short line of what in it the story touches. The story's prompt names those elements and links to them in the same form. Only elements the drafter found are cited, and where they point is the only thing that is Piton source: the drafter still learns what the spec says only from the compiled reference or from what `piton slice` prints, never by reading the Piton source, as the [ModeSystemPrompts](../prompt-mode/mode-system-prompts/index.md#mode-system-prompts) says, and works out each element's source from what `piton slice` prints for it, which names the file each declaration is in.
149-The application checks each link against the Piton source when the proposal arrives: the file has to exist under the spec location, and has to define the named element, the anchor declared in it and, for a property, that property on it, as `piton slice` would find it. A link that fails either check is dropped, with a muted note on the card saying which link it dropped and why, "no such file" or "the file doesn't define Planning.sprint". Each citation, on the proposal card and in an accepted story's "Spec" list, opens that place in the Piton source, the file in the editor at the element's declaration, as the [OpenFile](../open-file/index.md#open-file) opens a file.
150-Where no spec element matches part of a story, as a request for something the spec doesn't describe yet, the story says so instead of inventing one: its `spec` list holds an entry reading "No match", with what was looked for, and its prompt says plainly that the spec has nothing on it yet, so the change is new. A proposal with no element at all, and no "No match", isn't shown as a story: its card says the drafter cited no spec, in the theme's warning colour, with "Draft again", which runs the draft for that story once more.
151-The citations are shown on the proposal's card, beneath its prompt, as a short list, each element's name, as `Planning.sprint`, and its file, muted, as a link that opens that place in the Piton source, a "No match" entry muted with a small marker, and are kept with the story once it is accepted, listed in the open story beneath its steps as "Spec", and shown in its card's tooltip. They can be edited with the story, removed, or added to by hand: "Add citation" opens a picker of the spec's elements, its anchors and their properties, found from the Piton source and searched by name, never from the compiled reference, and adds the one picked in the same form, checked as a proposal's are.
152-Each citation says what kind of element it is, "Requirement", "Behaviour", "Name", or "Section", shown as a small muted label before it, and quotes the few words of the spec it rests on, so what the story relies on can be checked at a glance. Grounding holds for every proposal, however it came: from Draft stories, from research that ends with proposals, and from an Ask answer's `suspense-story` block, each checked and flagged the same way when it arrives.

154-## Proposal Cards
155-
156-Each proposal is a card in the panel, in the order given: a label at its top, "New story" in the theme's accent, or, for an addition, "Add to this story" where it targets the story the research or draft was started from, and "Add to" and the story's title otherwise, in the story's own mode's colour, so where it will go is always read before anything else on the card; its title, its mode pill, and its prompt beneath, clipped at four lines with "Read more"; and three buttons along its foot, "Accept", "Edit", and "Reject", reachable by Tab and labelled for a screen reader. Edit opens the card's title, prompt, mode, and target in place, the target a menu of "New story" and each story, to be changed before it is accepted; editing doesn't accept it. Reject removes the card, with a short "Undo" in its place for a few seconds.
157-Accept records it in the plan straight away, saved as any change to the plan is: a new story goes into the Backlog, at its end, in its mode, marked "From agent" with a small sparkle beside its title until it is first opened, its spec citations kept with it, whether it was accepted on its own card or with "Accept all", and whether it came from drafting, research, or an Ask answer. Accepting never adds anything to Next sprint, so it never starts a sprint, however Start automatically is set; an accepted story reaches a sprint only once it is moved to Next sprint, as the sprint says; an addition is added to the named story as a new step at its end, as its steps say, its attachments with it, and the story is marked "Updated by agent" until it is next opened, the new step shown highlighted the first time it is; where the story no longer exists by then, the step isn't added, as the steps say, and the card stays, its target changed to "New story", to be accepted again. A story already sent can't be added to: its proposals offer only "New story". Each card, once decided, goes from the panel, and an accepted one leaves a one-line muted note in its place, "Added to Backlog" or "Added to <story>", with a link that opens the story where it is, in the Backlog or its section.
158-Ask answers can propose the same way: a `suspense-story` block in an answer is shown as a proposal card, with the same Accept, Edit, and Reject, its accepting recording it in the plan as above, so a story can come out of a conversation without anything written silently.

160-## Research
161-
162-The agent can be asked to research a topic before anything is planned or built: "Research", a button beside "Draft stories" on the Backlog's heading, and "Research" in an open story's menu, open a box labelled "What should be looked into?". Research runs as a read-only investigation, a Question run, as the [Sandbox](../sandbox/index.md#sandbox) says, which can read the project and the web and change nothing, given the research prompt of the [BakedSystemPrompts](../harness-integration/baked-prompts/index.md#baked-system-prompts), which asks for findings, not changes: what was found, where, with links to the files and lines it read, what it is unsure of, and what it would suggest, and it may end with proposals, as the proposals say. Its box and its run are shown in the side panel, replacing whatever it held, as drafting's are, its live line of what it is reading, a Stop button, and its spinner, and its proposals in the same cards with the same buttons as drafting's, with Accept all and Reject all at its foot.
163-Its findings are a document of their own, "Research: <topic>", rendered as markdown with its links clickable, shown in the Findings column, the board's last, after Done, newest first, each a row with its topic, when it ran, and its state, and opening in a body tab of its own to read whole. Findings started from a story are also listed in that story, beneath its steps. They are saved with the plan, in `.suspense/planning/research/`, a markdown file each, so they can be read again later, and can be deleted from their row's menu.
